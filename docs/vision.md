# Vision

## The promise

Your first three survivors arrive at a failing outpost. Months later, the home they built supplies a neighboring settlement, shelters a rescued rival, and anchors an alliance. Their personalities and choices matter as much as the buildings.

The world is the Drowned Frontier: a planet flooded by an ancient attempt to harness a force beneath its crust. Settlements reclaim islands, colossal roots, floating wrecks, and sealed ruins. The setting connects industrial survival, luminous nature, and unsettling discoveries.

## Design pillars

1. **People create the story.** Needs, skills, injuries, relationships, and memories affect decisions and survival.
2. **Systems create depth.** Power affects pumps; flooding affects storage; shortages affect morale; morale affects work.
3. **Neighbors matter.** Specialization, distance, contracts, and mutual defense make other players valuable.
4. **Loss is understandable.** Explain causes, expose risks, and provide ways to recover. No arbitrary colony destruction while someone sleeps.
5. **The browser is the front door.** A shared link, readable UI, reasonable downloads, and reconnect support are core requirements.

## Three environments, one world

| Region | Identity | Useful exports | Pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canopy | Forests on islands and immense roots | Food, fibers, medicine | Storms, fire, wildlife |
| Shallows | Reefs, floating docks, ruined industry | Salvage, machinery, transport | Exposure, piracy, corrosion |
| Deep | Sealed research and mining habitats | Rare minerals, advanced technology | Oxygen, pressure, leaks |

Environment-specific simulations arrive gradually. The first playable region is the **Shallows**, with dry interiors and a damaged salvage outpost. Full liquid simulation, deep pressure, and living architecture are expansion work.

## Presentation

- Top-down 2D with painterly terrain, crisp silhouettes, restrained animation, layered light, and small expressive colonists.
- Ocean navy, oxidized teal, warm amber interiors, and selective bioluminescent accents. Danger uses shape and labels as well as color.
- Zoom levels: regional map, settlement, colonist inspection. Region and settlement are views of the same authoritative world, not separate economies.
- Sound communicates events and atmosphere; essential information remains available visually.
- Use clearly labeled placeholder art during technical prototyping. Art direction quality is evaluated separately from simulation completeness.

## Initial slice and future scope

Initial target: 2–4 players, eight settlement sites, three colonists per player, 64×64 local maps, one shared salvage location, one origin scenario, and one biome. These are test budgets, not measured capacity claims.

Later: multiple origins, regional production chains, relationships, research, factions, contract systems, scheduled wars, underwater habitats, world events, and mobile crawler outposts.

The first slice excludes seamless planetary rendering, massive player counts, all three biomes, elaborate fluid physics, ship construction, mobile cities, real-money trading, and permanent home conquest. Depth grows from a proven core.

## Success questions

- Can a new player explain why a colonist is idle and fix it?
- Does the first hour produce a memorable decision rather than only construction clicks?
- Do two settlements have a practical reason to trade?
- Can two players contest a resource without destroying each other's ability to continue?
- Does reconnecting preserve orders, ownership, and possessions accurately?
